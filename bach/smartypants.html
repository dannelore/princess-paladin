The Rules of Engagement

Read Before You Present

  1. Pick something you have entirely too much to say about

    This is not a work presentation.

    The more niche, unnecessary, or passionately opinionated, the better.

  2. You're the expert because you said so

    No credentials required.

    Research is optional. Confidence is mandatory. Wildly specific evidence is encouraged.

  3. Make a short slideshow

    Aim for about 5–10 minutes.

    Use PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva — whatever lets you adequately communicate your brilliance.

  4. Visual aids are highly encouraged

    Photos. Charts. Rankings. Screenshots. Memes. Evidence exhibits.

    A graph you absolutely did not need to make is particularly welcome.

  5. Teach us something

    By the end, we should know something we didn't know before.

    Whether that information is remotely useful is irrelevant.

  6. Questions & arguments may occur

    Your audience is allowed to ask questions.

    They are also allowed to challenge your thesis. Defend it accordingly.

  7. Commit to the bit

    You are giving a TED Talk on the most important subject in the world.

    For the next ten minutes, everyone else simply has to accept that.

See you at the podium.

Slides due whenever you feel like it. Confidence due immediately.
